Grammar

Grammar

Four key ideas of Mundari grammar

Mundari word order is SOV — first the subject, then the object, and the verb right at the end. Nouns do not change form for gender or number; the work is done by postpositions (-𞓣𞓤 -re in, -𞓝𞓤 -te to / with, -𞓕𞓝𞓤 -ate from) and by small markers attached to the verb.

Pronouns — two forms of “we”

SingularDualPlural
I / we (including the listener)𞓕𞓘añ𞓕𞓒𞓕𞓔alaŋ𞓕𞓗𞓟abu
We (excluding the listener)—𞓕𞓒𞓚𞓔aliŋ𞓕𞓒𞓤ale
You𞓕𞓧am𞓕𞓗𞓤𞓨aben𞓕𞓑𞓤ape
He / she / they𞓕𞓤𞓙aeʔ𞓕𞓢𞓚𞓨akin𞓕𞓢𞓐ako

The biggest differences from Hindi and English: Mundari has a dual (a separate form for exactly two), and two words for “we” — 𞓕𞓗𞓟 abu when the listener is included, 𞓕𞓒𞓤 ale when not. For possession, add -𞓕𞓙 -aʔ: 𞓕𞓘-𞓕𞓙 añ-aʔ mine, 𞓕𞓧-𞓕𞓙 am-aʔ yours.

Building a verb

𞓖𞓐𞓧 jom = to eat. From this one root:

𞓖𞓐𞓧-𞓝𞓕-𞓨-𞓕 jom-ta-n-a — is eating (continuous tense)
𞓖𞓐𞓧-𞓢𞓤-𞓡-𞓕 jom-ke-d-a — has eaten (completed)
𞓕-𞓖𞓐𞓧 a-jom — to feed someone
𞓛𞓤𞓨𞓐𞓙-𞓝𞓕-𞓨-𞓕 senoʔ-ta-n-a — is going · 𞓛𞓤𞓨𞓐𞓦-𞓕 senog-a — will go / goes

How do you say “I have …”?

Mundari has no verb like “have”. You say — “is there a book with you?”:

𞓕𞓧-𞓝𞓕𞓙-𞓣𞓤 𞓢𞓚𞓝𞓕𞓗 𞓧𞓤𞓨𞓕𞓙?
Am-taʔ-re kitab menaʔ? — Do you have a book?
𞓕𞓘-𞓝𞓕𞓙-𞓣𞓤 𞓛𞓤𞓝𞓕-𞓢𞓐 𞓧𞓤𞓨𞓕𞓙-𞓢𞓐-𞓕.
Añ-taʔ-re seta-ko menaʔ-ko-a. — I have dogs.
The negative forms are different words: 𞓗𞓕𞓨𞓐𞓙 banoʔ (for inanimate things), 𞓗𞓕𞓔𞓦𞓕𞓚𞓙-𞓕 baŋgaiʔ-a (for living beings, “he/she is not there”).

Negation — 𞓢𞓕 “ka”

To say “no / not”, put 𞓢𞓕 ka before the verb: 𞓢𞓕-𞓘 𞓡𞓕𞓣𞓚-𞓕 ka-ñ dari-a “I cannot do it”; 𞓕𞓟𞓣𞓚 auri means “not yet / not so far”.

Question words

𞓐𞓢𞓐𞓤 okoe who · 𞓢𞓕𞓨𞓕 cana what · 𞓢𞓐𞓣𞓤 kore where · 𞓠𞓚𞓧𞓝𞓕𞓔 cimtaŋ when · 𞓛𞓚𞓒𞓚𞓢𞓕𞓝𞓤 cilikate why · 𞓛𞓚𞓧𞓚𞓨𞓕𞓔 ciminaŋ how much. Yes–no questions end with 𞓛𞓚? ci?

Number — -𞓢𞓚𞓨 / -𞓢𞓐 -kin / -ko

𞓛𞓤𞓝𞓕 seta dog → 𞓛𞓤𞓝𞓕-𞓢𞓚𞓨 seta-kin two dogs → 𞓛𞓤𞓝𞓕-𞓢𞓐 seta-ko dogs (many). Gender is only living vs non-living — there is no masculine / feminine.
