Introduction

Language is an important part of community identity, memory and everyday life. In Mayurbhanj, several Adivasi languages exist alongside Odia and other regional languages. Mundari is part of this multilingual landscape. Preserving it requires attention to local speech, family use, education, oral literature and the choices of younger generations.

1. A multilingual district

Mayurbhanj includes speakers of languages belonging to different linguistic families. Mundari, Ho and Bhumij are associated with the Munda branch of Austroasiatic. Odia belongs to the Indo-Aryan branch. People may use more than one language depending on home, school, work, markets and public life.

The presence of related languages does not mean they are interchangeable. Mundari, Ho and Bhumij should be documented with their own vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation and community context.

2. Evidence from language and education work

Lokesh Prasad Dash's 2019 article describes an initiative in Mayurbhanj to improve children's reading skills through indigenous-language and bilingual supplementary materials. The account discusses the use of community participation in developing reading resources and identifies Mundari and Ho among languages facing reduced use in the context examined.

This article is a case study of an educational intervention. It is not a census of all speakers and should not be used to claim that every Mundari-speaking village has the same language situation.

3. What language preservation should record

A useful language archive should preserve more than word lists. It should record language in meaningful contexts:

  • greetings and everyday conversation;
  • family and kinship terms;
  • names of plants, animals and local places;
  • agriculture and seasonal activities;
  • songs, stories and proverbs;
  • traditional knowledge shared with permission;
  • children's speech and contemporary expressions.

For each entry, record the original form, script, pronunciation, meaning, example, location or variety, contributor and review status.

4. Bilingual and multilingual learning resources

Bilingual resources can help children connect familiar spoken language with reading and writing. Materials should be developed with local teachers, parents, fluent speakers and language workers. A translation should not be considered accurate simply because it is grammatically fluent; it must preserve the intended meaning and local usage.

Mundari Samaj can support:

  • short reading passages;
  • picture dictionaries;
  • audio pronunciation;
  • children's stories;
  • parallel English–Mundari and Odia–Mundari texts;
  • community-reviewed translations;
  • downloadable learning sheets.

The website should label the region or variety represented by each resource where known.

5. Respecting scripts and spelling variation

The archive should not assume that all Mundari speakers use one script or one spelling convention. Contributors should be able to record the script and spelling they use. If a standardised spelling is adopted for a particular collection, the editorial team should explain the convention and retain alternate forms where useful.

Unicode support is essential. The website should test the display of all supplied scripts on Android, iOS and desktop browsers.

6. A community-led preservation model

Language preservation works best when speakers help decide what should be recorded and how it should be used. Contributors should be able to choose attribution and give clear permission for publication, audio use and translation.

The archive should also distinguish:

  • community-contributed material;
  • editor-checked material;
  • language-specialist-reviewed material;
  • material awaiting verification.

Conclusion

Mundari language preservation in Mayurbhanj is part of a wider effort to sustain multilingual education, cultural memory and intergenerational communication. A digital archive can support this work by preserving local forms and making resources accessible, provided that community speakers remain central to the process.

Editorial notes

Do not label Mundari as extinct or universally endangered without current evidence.

Describe the specific study and area when discussing language shift.

Have fluent local Mundari speakers review all examples and translations.
