Introduction

Odisha is an important region for the study of Munda communities and Munda languages. Munda communities live in several districts, maintain varied local histories and participate in the state's social, cultural and economic life. The history of these communities cannot be understood through one district or one description alone.

This article introduces the available institutional evidence and explains how a more detailed Odisha Munda history can be documented responsibly.

1. Odisha's diverse Munda-speaking landscape

The Munda branch belongs to the Austroasiatic language family. In Odisha, several Scheduled Tribe communities speak languages classified within the Munda branch. These include communities associated with Mundari, Ho, Santali, Kharia, Sora, Juang and other languages.

The Odisha SCST Research and Training Institute's publication Impact of Pandemics on Tribal Culture of Odisha describes the state's Scheduled Tribes through broad ethno-linguistic groupings. It identifies a number of communities as Munda-language speakers. This is a linguistic classification, not a statement that all those communities share one ethnic identity or one language.

Mundari, Ho and Santali are related languages, but they are distinct. Their speakers' histories and local identities should be documented separately.

2. Where official profiles identify Munda communities

The Odisha State Tribal Museum's Munda profile lists Sundargarh, Sambalpur, Keonjhar and adjoining districts as the region of the Munda community. Its separate Mundari profile identifies Mundari-speaking communities in Sundargarh, Mayurbhanj and parts of Keonjhar.

The SCSTRTI's Demographic Profile of Scheduled Tribes in Odisha also lists Munda and Mundari among communities found in a number of tribal sub-plan blocks in Sundargarh district. Its table records the source basis as an SCSTRTI Universal Bench Mark Survey from 1978–79 and Census of India 1971 police-station-wise distribution. This is historical administrative evidence, not a current village register.

These sources provide a starting point. They should not be treated as an exhaustive or current map of all Munda households.

3. Regional differences matter

Munda families in Sundargarh, Keonjhar, Sambalpur and Mayurbhanj may have different settlement histories, language practices, livelihood experiences and relationships with neighbouring communities.

A custom recorded in one location should not automatically be described as a custom of every Munda community in Odisha. The same principle applies to clan names, marriage practices, village leadership, religious traditions and festival terminology.

Mundari Samaj should create district-specific records and identify the location to which each account applies.

4. Work, education and changing livelihoods

Institutional descriptions of the Munda community in Odisha refer to settled cultivation and industrial labour. These descriptions provide a broad picture, but contemporary livelihoods are diverse and change over time.

Research should document agriculture, wage labour, industrial employment, education, migration, public service, business, arts and other occupations without portraying the community through a single economic stereotype.

5. What the records do not yet tell us

A district list does not explain when a village was founded, how a family arrived, what local name residents use for themselves or how language use has changed across generations.

To answer these questions, the archive needs:

  • village-level oral histories;
  • old land, revenue and settlement documents where accessible;
  • district gazetteers and census records;
  • local school and community publications;
  • interviews with elders and language speakers;
  • records of migration and family connections, with consent.

Conclusion

Munda communities are part of Odisha's diverse Adivasi history. Institutional profiles identify important regions, but a fuller account must be built through district-level and village-level research. Mundari Samaj can contribute by preserving local voices, accurate language records and carefully sourced histories.

Editorial notes

Check the current official district and block names before adding map filters.

Do not merge Munda and Mundari population totals.

Obtain local review from Munda community members in each district represented.
