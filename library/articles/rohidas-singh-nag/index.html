Rohidas Singh Nag (1934–2012) — Ol Guru, creator of Mundari Bani

2026-11-03 · 6 min read · People & Personalities · Mundari Samaj Editorial Desk

Full name: Rohidas Singh Nag · Born: 5 February 1934, Chandua village, Mayurbhanj district, Odisha · Died: 30 December 2012, Salbani, Mayurbhanj (aged 78) · Occupation: writer and linguist · Known for: inventing the Mundari Bani script · Revered as: Ol Guru

A schoolboy's invention, 1949

In 1949, Rohidas Singh Nag was a class III student in Mayurbhanj. Mundari was spoken all around him but had no script of its own — it was written, when written at all, in Devanagari, Bengali, Odia or Roman letters. The young student began designing letters for the sounds of his language and wrote the first Mundari Bani alphabet on the wall of his school, using clay.

Thirty-five letters, 1953

He kept working through his school years. By 1953 the set had grown to 35 characters — a complete first form of the script, its letterforms drawn, as they still suggest today, from shapes found in nature and daily life.

The 27-letter script, 1980

Nag later simplified his own creation. In 1980 a set of 27 letters was selected for use — the core alphabet taught today. That same year he presented the script to the then Chief Minister of Odisha, Shri J. B. Pattnaik, and submitted a memorandum seeking constitutional recognition for the Munda language. In 1996 he was felicitated by the Academy of Tribal Languages and Culture.

Carrying the demand to Delhi, 1999

In 1999, Nag went with others to submit a memorandum to the President of India, again appealing for constitutional recognition of the Munda language. Around him, organisations such as the Mundari Samaj Sanwar Jamda of Poda Astia, Mayurbhanj, pressed the community's wider demands: a place for Mundari in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, broadcasts on All India Radio, and a department for the language at North Odisha University.

The 2008 reform

In 2008 the Bharat Munda Samaj and the Mundari Samaj Sanwar Jamda worked with Nag himself to reform the script: letterforms that were confusing or hard to read and write were restyled, and new glyphs were added. This reformed version is the script in use today.

Into Unicode, 2022

A formal proposal to encode Mundari Bani in the Unicode Standard was prepared by Lawrence Wolf-Sonkin and Biswajit Mandal in 2021. The script was accepted and published in Unicode 15.0 in September 2022 (block U+1E4D0–U+1E4FF) — ten years after Nag's death. That is why Mundari Bani can now be typed on phones and computers, and why it appears beside every Mundari word on this website.

Legacy

Nag is remembered as Ol Guru — the teacher. A writer and poet as well as the script's creator, he spent more than six decades on a single conviction: that a community survives through its language, and a language survives through its own script. The full alphabet chart, with every letter, is on this site's Script page.
