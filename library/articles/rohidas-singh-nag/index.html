Early life and vision

Rohidas Singh Nag, respectfully remembered as Ol Guru Rohidas Singh Nag, was the creator of Mundari Bani, an independent writing system developed for the Mundari language.

He was born in 1934 in Mayurbhanj district of Odisha. His contribution became an important chapter in the history of Mundari language and literature.

Mundari is an Austroasiatic language spoken by Munda communities, particularly in Odisha, Jharkhand and West Bengal. It has a rich oral tradition, including songs, stories, customs and traditional knowledge.

Before Mundari Bani, Mundari was also written using scripts such as Odia, Devanagari, Bengali and Latin. Rohidas Singh Nag worked to develop a writing system specifically for the language.

The creation of Mundari Bani

According to the Unicode documentation, Rohidas Singh Nag began developing the earliest form of Mundari Bani during his school years in the early 1950s.

He experimented with letters and gradually developed a writing system. Over the years, he refined the characters to make the script more suitable for writing the sounds of Mundari.

By the early 1980s, he had started sharing his writing system with the Munda community.

A manuscript dated 10 July 1982, documented in the Unicode proposal, preserves an earlier form of the script and provides historical evidence of its development.

A language carries the memory of a people. A writing system gives those memories another way to reach future generations.

Development and spread

Creating a script was only the beginning. For Mundari Bani to become useful, people needed to learn it, write with it and use it in books and educational activities.

Community organisations and supporters of Mundari literature helped spread the script through educational and cultural activities.

By 1994, Mundari Bani had reached a wider audience in Odisha.

In 2004, ten Mundari Bani schools opened in Odisha, according to the historical timeline documented in the Unicode proposal.

Another significant achievement came with the publication of the first book written in Mundari Bani, titled Mundari Bani Hisir, in 2004, under the auspices of Bharat Munda Samaj.

The 2008 reform of Mundari Bani

In 2008, an important development took place in the history of the script.

Bharat Munda Samaj and Mundari Samaj Sanwar Jamda worked together with Rohidas Singh Nag to reform and improve Mundari Bani.

Some character shapes were modified to make them easier to distinguish and write. The revised version also introduced an additional basic letter and a sign known as Tong.

The development of computer fonts also helped make the script more accessible for digital writing and publishing.

Important distinction: Mundari is the language, while Mundari Bani is its writing system. Nag Mundari is the technical name used for the script in Unicode.

Understanding the Nag Mundari script

Mundari Bani is an alphabetic writing system created to represent the sounds of the Mundari language.

Some of its important characteristics include:

  • It is written from left to right.
  • It has separate vowel and consonant letters.
  • Its standard form contains 27 basic letters.
  • It uses additional signs, including Tong, for specific writing functions.
  • It has its own Unicode block for digital representation.

International digital recognition: Unicode 15.0

A major milestone in the digital history of Mundari Bani came on 13 September 2022.

On this date, the Unicode Consortium released Unicode Standard Version 15.0, which included the Nag Mundari script.

The script received its own Unicode block:

Nag Mundari: U+1E4D0–U+1E4FF

Unicode encoding makes it possible for computers and compatible digital systems to represent and process the characters of Nag Mundari.

This achievement creates opportunities for digital writing, educational resources, electronic publishing and the preservation of Mundari-language materials.

The name Nag Mundari also recognises Rohidas Singh Nag's contribution as the creator of the script.

Legacy of Ol Guru

Rohidas Singh Nag passed away in 2012, but his contribution continues to be remembered through the writing system he developed.

His work represents an important part of the wider effort to preserve indigenous languages, literature and cultural knowledge.

Mundari Bani demonstrates how individuals and community organisations can contribute to documenting and strengthening their mother tongue.

Today, the script has a place in educational materials, books and digital resources. Its inclusion in Unicode has also created a foundation for wider digital accessibility.

His legacy is not limited to the creation of an alphabet. It is connected to the continuing effort to make Mundari language, literature and knowledge accessible to future generations.

A contribution worth remembering

A language carries the memories, knowledge, traditions and identity of its speakers. Preserving a language also means creating opportunities for it to be written, taught, documented and used by future generations.

Ol Guru Rohidas Singh Nag made a lasting contribution to that effort by creating Mundari Bani.

His work deserves recognition in the history of the Munda community and the wider history of indigenous writing systems in India.
