Adi Vaani: India's first AI translator for tribal languages launches — with Mundari

2025-09-01 · Language technology

On 1 September 2025, at the Dr Ambedkar International Centre in New Delhi, Minister of State for Tribal Affairs Durgadas Uikey launched the beta version of Adi Vaani — described by the Press Information Bureau as India's first AI-powered translation tool for tribal languages, developed under the banner of Janjatiya Gaurav Varsh. Its first four languages were Santali, Bhili, Mundari (from Jharkhand) and Gondi. The platform offers text and speech translation between Hindi, English and the tribal languages, OCR for digitising tribal manuscripts and primers, bilingual dictionaries and language-learning material, and was built by a national consortium led by IIT Delhi with BITS Pilani, IIIT Hyderabad, IIIT Nava Raipur and the Tribal Research Institutes, using the NLLB and IndicTrans2 AI models with community-validated data. President Droupadi Murmu later called the launch “an important step towards language and education transformation in tribal areas”. Editor's note: Adi Vaani's Mundari output is machine translation and, as the platform itself indicates, should be reviewed by speakers before use. Sources: Press Information Bureau releases, 1 and 9 September 2025; Lok Sabha answer, 29 January 2026.
